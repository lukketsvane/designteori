Create one finished square (1:1) drawing on pure white paper, edge to edge. Black pen only. An original page of visual thinking about a historical act of measurement: a person becomes a catalogue finding, while the supposed natural boundary comes from the measurer’s own hand. Make the argument through what the ink lines actually do. Three drawings of different sizes share an open sheet; no panels, title or page number. Leave generous, irregular intervals of untouched white.

UPPER LEFT, centred about one quarter across and one fifth down: a little piece of earth in section, just two loose strata bending around a smooth empty hollow. Follow the two strata to the right: without a break they straighten and turn corners, becoming the upper and lower rules of a single narrow data-entry field. A few unnumbered measuring ticks mark the transition. Earth and record are one object, not two pictures connected by an arrow. Handwrite “BRUKARINNSIKT” inside the field, small enough to fit. Below and to the left write “SAMISKE GRAVER” with a short oblique leader ending in the hollow.

UPPER RIGHT, offset lower, centred about three quarters across and one third down: a small curved frontal bone plate seen obliquely, identifiable by its brow edge and a single short meandering suture. Its curved right edge unfolds and squares off into a flat catalogue tab, made of the bone itself. Write “EIT FUNN” in black ink directly across that flat end. No face or jaw below the brow: the white paper there is essential to the drawing. Just below and a little right, handwrite “KVAR MUNN VAR BORTE.” A fine bent pointer from the words stops in that empty white space, *not* on the bone.

LOWER HALF, the largest drawing: at the left, a plainly drawn human hand in side view holds the sliding carriage of a simple vernier caliper between thumb and bent forefinger. Two short wrist contours trail off into white. Make the tool legible with economical strokes: a narrow horizontal beam, a few unnumbered scale ticks, one fixed jaw and one sliding jaw, empty paper between the jaws. Show just three nested fingerprint ridges on the pad of the gripping thumb. The outer ridge continues along the sliding jaw and off its tip as one uninterrupted black pen line. Follow it across the otherwise empty lower middle: it bends, travels to the lower right and turns back on itself to make an irregular map boundary. Inside that outline repeat the thumb’s other two ridges, much enlarged. The apparent map is unmistakably an imprint of the hand, not a line found on the bone. Beneath the hand, place the two-line note “HANDA SOM HELD” / “SKYVELÆRET”, with a short leader to the grip; the slash denotes a line break and is NOT written. In the open space above the first bend write “TEIKNA EI GRENSE” with a little downward pointer to the stroke. Close beside the finished outline at lower right write “KALLA GRENSA NATUR.” with a small leader touching its edge.

Draw lightly and decisively in real-looking freehand black ink: thin, slightly wavering contours, occasional pressure changes at a turn, barely any interior detail. The handwritten notes are modest and legible, in informal uneven UPPERCASE on slightly wandering baselines, not typeset lettering. The quoted Nynorsk annotations are the ONLY visible words; transcribe them exactly once, including the Æ in SKYVELÆRET. Do not write quotation marks, layout instructions, English, labels of your own, numerals or a signature. No grey, fills, shading, hatching, paper grain, shadows or colour.
