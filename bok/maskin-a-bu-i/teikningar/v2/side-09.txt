Create one original square 1:1 page of speculative pen drawings. The image is the white page itself, seen flat and straight on. Black ink only. Use thin, slightly wavering freehand contours, the occasional tiny overshoot where two strokes meet, and small, legible handwritten CAPITAL letters. Objects are described by very few marks. Think by changing what a line means halfway along its length. Four unboxed studies of different sizes sit asymmetrically in ample untouched white space; no panel grid, timeline, heading or decorative border. Use the references for the economy of the marks, not for any of their figures or compositions.

UPPER LEFT, small, around one fifth of the way across the sheet: two close upright wall strokes pinch the back of a little chair seen from the side. Its bent seat escapes the narrow space and curls back into a D-shaped pull handle. A single downward force arrow meets the seat; beneath the seat the very same stroke becomes the leg that carries the load. Handwrite “HEILE TYNGDA SI” just above and to the left of the arrow, as its annotation. Keep this study as spare as a margin sketch.

UPPER RIGHT, a slightly larger opened specimen: the hollow oblong shell of a transformer hall, drawn in a loose oblique cutaway. Two low brick flanks remain standing; only a handful of staggered mortar joints are needed. A narrow cut through the middle is fitted with a clear, unfilled pane held between two thin aluminium channels. From the vacated interior a copper winding is drawn out in two slack turns, one end hanging free. Under the floor, a single outlined oil drop passes through a little three-slot drain. Give the old shell one distinctive clipped corner. Handwrite “TEGLEN FEKK STÅ” above the standing brick with a short leader to it; “GLAS OG ALUMINIUM” beside the cut with a fine pointer to the channel; “KOPAREN RØSKA UT” tucked below the loose wire. Leave white paper visible through the building and through the pane.

MID-LEFT, lower than the hall and well separated from it: a broad rectangular workshop doorway standing on its own, with a tiny blank plaque pinned to one jamb. One object straddles the threshold. Outside, it has the thickness and stepped edges of a heavy stack of transformer laminations; follow its top edge THROUGH the doorway and it has become a thin tilted drafting board, with a little T-square resting on it and two simple trestle strokes unfolding underneath. Make the shared edge unmistakable: the portal stays the same, what passes through it changes. Put “VERKSTADPORTEN” to the left with a short leader to the jamb, and “TEIKNEBORDA” just beyond the tilted board, pointing to its edge.

LOWER CENTRE-RIGHT, the concluding and largest drawing: a SINGLE oblong iron transformer core, a continuous rectangular ring with a generous white window through it. Echo the clipped corner of the earlier building so this is also the plan of the school. Two distinct wire windings embrace opposite limbs of this SAME core, three relaxed turns on each, with the upper and lower connecting rails plainly visible. The free end of the left winding turns two corners and becomes a tiny architectural room plan with an opening and a door-swing arc. The right winding's free end bends into the ergonomic chair-and-handle profile from the first study. These are transformations of the wires themselves, not detached icons. A little handwritten “A” points to the left winding and “B” to the right. In the open space to the lower left of the core, put a small loose two-line handwritten key: “A  ARKITEKTUR” then “B  DESIGN”. Just under the core, slightly off to one side, set the quiet handwritten note “TO VIKLINGAR KRING SAME KJERNEN.”, breaking after KRING if needed.
