Make one finished square (1:1) page of original speculative pen drawings. The image is the white page itself, seen flat. Pure black ink on pure white: fine, slightly wavering freehand contours, little variations of nib pressure, unfilled interiors. No grey, shading, hatching, colour, paper texture, panel borders or typesetting. Let the white paper do much of the thinking. Use the supplied pages as references for the economy of marks, not as sources of figures or arrangements. The idea of this new page: a use can be slipped into a thing from outside and then mistaken for something found there.

Arrange five drawings of unequal size, loosely, with generous gaps and margins:

• Upper left: a little wooden chair has tipped onto its side. Beside it, the intended sitting position remains upright in midair: just a broken-line right angle for the absent seat and a small load arrow pointing down onto empty white paper. The actual chair has fallen clear of the diagram. Write “FORM FØLGJER FUNKSJON.” in small handwriting beside the empty angle, with a fine leader to it. This is a marginal note, not a title.

• Upper right, a little higher and smaller: a fragment of floor plan, two rooms sharing a wall with an open door. At the hinge, the door's swing arc lifts out of the plan and its end becomes a bent door lever in profile. A loosely outlined open hand turns away; clear white space between fingers and lever. Just under the hand write “EI HAND KAN NEKTE Å GRIPE.” on two short lines. Point the note into the gap rather than at the hand.

• Across the middle, the principal and largest experiment: draw an irregular, soft-edged piece of folded paper with two slots, like a belt keeper. From the left a thumb and fingertip feed a long, slightly buckled paper strip into one slot. It disappears behind the fold and reappears at the right slot, ending in a broad, unmistakable diagram-arrow head. It is ONE material strip threaded through, not three symbols connected by arrows. Handwrite “FUNKSJON” on its exposed tail. Above the entering fold put the little note “UTANFRÅ” with a short pointer; at the emerging arrowhead put “LESE HAN UT ATT”. Let this figure tilt gently across the white field.

• Lower left: in plan, two freely meandering riverbanks surround one small island. Both banks pinch through a removable paper cuff and continue, without a break, as the two walls of a narrow cooling pipe bent into two hairpin turns. No landscape or machinery around it. Put a tiny handwritten A by the cuff.

• Lower right, offset slightly downward: a small student bent over an open notebook, suggested by an oval, shoulder and one attentive hand. The notebook's right leaf folds outward; its gutter continues as the seam of a little dispatch carton, its pages becoming the flaps. One continuous paper metamorphosis, not a person inside a box. Put a tiny B at the turning crease. Beneath the river, in the remaining white pocket, a loose two-line handwritten key:
“A  EI ELV VERT KJØLEVATN,”
“B  EIN STUDENT VERT LEVERANSE.”

Only the quoted inscriptions and the two figure letters may appear. No quotation marks. Keep lettering legible, small, informal, ALL UPPERCASE, in the same pen as the drawings; preserve the exact Nynorsk spelling, especially Ø and Å. No header, page number, signature, extra words or borrowed reference motifs.
