Make one finished SQUARE 1:1 page of speculative drawing. Flat, pure white paper and black pen only. Use the supplied pages as a reference for the economy of the marks: a few slightly tremulous freehand contours, small handwritten observations beside things, a drawing that thinks by letting one material turn into another. Invent all the objects and their arrangement anew. Keep generous, irregular intervals of untouched paper. No panels, border, heading, page number, grey, shading, hatching, paper texture or shadows. Not a poster, engineering blueprint or historical scene.

UPPER LEFT, occupying roughly the left two-fifths of the upper third: draw a squat stationary workshop motor on a low slice of concrete, in a spare three-quarter view. Two bolted feet, a cylinder, a little flywheel on the near end suffice. The flywheel rim comes loose as a pen line: it makes three turns in almost exactly the same place, bends down, and tightens into the screw thread of the anchor bolt inside the concrete. This must read as ONE changing line, rotation becoming the very thing that holds the motor still. Leave the concrete white inside so the bolt can be seen. In the white space to the right of the wheel write, on two short lines, “PÅ FULLT TURTAL,
TIME ETTER TIME,” with a short leader to the turns. Lower down, level with the embedded bolt, place “UTAN Å FLYTTE SEG
EIN MILLIMETER.” with a fine pointer to its fixed tip.

MIDDLE, offset to the RIGHT, separate from the motor: draw a single object that is half order sheet, half iron door hinge, lying slightly askew. The broad left leaf is thin paper, with a curling corner, a date handwritten on it, “HAUSTEN 1944”, and two otherwise empty ruled fields. The fold of the sheet rolls into three hinge knuckles. Beyond that fold the same sheet has become a narrow iron hinge leaf with two clean screw holes. Let the line of a paper ruling become the edge of the iron. Just above the iron leaf write “KVA DØRA TRENG AV JERN” and point to a screw hole. In the open space to the LEFT of the sheet write “IKKJE KVEN SOM FÅR GÅ
GJENNOM HENNE.” Its quiet leader ends in the utterly blank field of the form. The empty paper matters as much as the metal.

LOWER THIRD: two small, widely separated enquiries. On the lower left, a loose two-hole iron fitting seen edge-on at a slight angle; a trail leaves one hole, breaks into three diminishing dashes, and is lost in white space without reaching anything. Put a small handwritten “A” beside its vanishing end. On the lower right, a very simple overhead plan of a closed door: two short hollow wall stubs, the narrow shut door leaf spanning the gap, and a small lock tongue visibly holding its end to the jamb. A single curved line of travel approaches and stops flush against the near face. Absolutely no line emerges on the other side. Put “B” next to that point of contact. Do NOT connect these two studies: where the fitting went is unknown; what a locked door does is known.

Below these two small studies, toward the left, a loose two-line handwritten key, no title:
A   EG VEIT IKKJE KVAR DEI BESLAGA HAMNA,
B   MEN EG VEIT KORLEIS EI LÅST DØR VERKAR.

All lettering must be modest-sized but clearly legible, informal hand-printed UPPERCASE in the same black pen, with natural spacing and careful Norwegian Å and Ø. Render ONLY the specified Norwegian inscriptions and the letters A and B; no quotation marks, no other text, no filler on the form. No people, suffering, war emblems or military imagery. Leave a clear white margin on all four sides.
